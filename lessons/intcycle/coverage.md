# Intelligence Cycle coverage

This standalone quiz has 25 easy, 25 medium and 25 hard questions. All seven configured categories occur in every bank. The questions apply the lesson to fictional Philippine Army classroom reviews, tabletop planning and disaster-response support. They do not form a combined Module 5 examination.

## Ambiguities and exclusions

- Slide 11 compresses the distinction into raw information versus processed intelligence. Questions use the explicit stage definitions on slides 12–13: translation, sorting or gaining access makes an input usable but does not by itself establish a validated intelligence conclusion.
- Slide 17 uses interpretation and pattern language in processing. Questions distinguish preparing usable meaning or flagging an anomaly from interpreting its significance and producing a decision-support assessment on slides 13 and 18–19.
- Slide 10's broad knowledge domains are treated as context for understanding and decisions. No questions teach assessment of an adversary's capabilities or intentions, tracking, targeting or other operational workflows.
- Slide 14's asset coordination is limited to generic staff review priorities in fictional exercises. Slide 15–18 signal content is limited to taxonomy, conceptual boundaries and artificial data reasoning. No frequencies, tactical locations, live/private military information, interception procedures, exploitation optimization or decryption instructions appear.
- Administrative, outline and classroom-management slides 1–9 and 22–27 are excluded. No item asks learners to recall a slide number or answer an administrative or safety-slide fact.
- Confidence and limitations are expressed as ordinary evidence-bounded reasoning, not as an external confidence formula or a newly introduced doctrinal rating scheme.

## Difficulty and audit

Easy questions apply one principal distinction to a short situation. Medium questions add a conflict, reporting gap, changed need or handoff problem. Hard questions combine competing considerations such as incomplete evidence and decision timing, changed requirements and reusable work, or sharing value and access restrictions.

The author reviewed the answer and nearest tempting alternative for each item. Options have four distinct strings and a single best supported answer. Explanations have two or three concise sentences and 35–70 words. Prompt and explanation strings are unique across the 75 questions. Answer positions are mixed rather than following the draft's repeating sequence.
