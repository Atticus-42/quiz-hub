# Radio Direction Finding coverage

Source basis: the previously inspected 36-slide **3. IMM Radio Direction Finder (Revised).pptx**, using its verified slide extraction and visual checks. Slide numbers below refer to the original deck, not a renumbered excerpt. The source file was no longer present at its original Downloads path during bank authoring; the retained, verified slide extraction supplied the source basis. No additional factual source was introduced.

This standalone lesson has 25 easy, 25 medium, and 25 hard questions. It is one of the two standalone Module 5 lesson quizzes; these banks do not define a combined module exam. All scenarios are authorized Philippine Army classroom, training, or tabletop discussions with non-sensitive, simulated information.

## Source boundaries and exclusions

- Slides 4 and 32 list components, calibration, system parameters, and error mitigation in outlines. The supplied teaching slides do not give detailed procedures for those topics. They are not assessed as taught practical skills, and this bank does not claim complete calibration, error, component, or parameter coverage. These omissions are documented here rather than assessed as procedures.
- Slide 25 names triangulation as a training concept. The questions may distinguish that mention from an actual position finding, but do not teach or test a triangulation workflow, site placement, bearing calculation, equipment setup, or calibration sequence.
- Slides 27 and 29 both describe phase-difference comparisons. Phase comparison and interferometric RDF are therefore never keyed as mutually exclusive alternatives. Comparisons use their shared phase principle and contrast it with the timing quantity on slide 28.
- Slide 30 defines DOA as an azimuthal arrival angle relative to the RDF system. The limitation that a single DOA does not independently establish distance, a precise geographic point, equipment model, ownership, identity, authorization, or intent is a bounded inference from that directional definition, not a quotation or a claim that the deck explicitly enumerates all those limitations. Related slides supply the relevant application or source context.
- Slide 20 uses the EPIRB label too broadly for aircraft, ship, and individual beacon examples. Items use generic distress-beacon wording; specific beacon subtype names and distinctions are omitted rather than teaching the overgeneralization.
- Slides 21, 23, and 31 discuss intelligence or surveillance applications. Operational collection, enemy tracking, targeting, border surveillance optimization, interception, and decryption are excluded. Slides 17–18 supply broad source examples only; no equipment recognition, waveform identification, or military source exploitation is assessed.
- Slides 9–10 are image-only safety material; slides 34–35 are feedback and thanks material, as previously visually checked. They are not RDF conceptual teaching content and do not supply quiz claims.
- Responsible use here means retaining the evidence's actual scope and using non-sensitive simulated classroom reports. The bank does not invent a detailed data-handling regulation or attribute one to the deck.
