SOAC 52–26 · Bandwidth Brothers

Reviewer 101

Start with a quick briefing, then practice making a decision and explain it in your own words.

Module 1 · Leadership

  • Military Leadership Attributes

    Recall the three leader attributes and use observable behavior to identify which one a leader needs to strengthen.

  • Military Leadership Competencies

    Distinguish leading, developing, and achieving by the purpose of the leader's action.

  • Company Management

    Connect company command authority with day-to-day responsibility for people, training, operations, and resources.

  • Mission Command

    Explain how leaders apply mission command and choose a degree of control that fits the task, team, and risk.

  • Ethical and Transformational Leadership

    Apply the three ethical lenses to a decision under pressure and recognize the Four I's of transformational leadership.

  • NCO Empowerment

    Distinguish NCO duties, responsibility, and authority, then match an inspection or correction to the actual training need.

  • Effective Communication 2

    Use the communication process to diagnose misunderstandings and choose a method that lets the receiver act correctly.

Module 2 · Army Operating Concept

  • Field Artillery Operations

    Recall the four basic field artillery tasks and explain how responsive support depends on a coordinated system.

  • Armor Operations

    Distinguish the roles of mechanized infantry, cavalry, and tanks and assess the conditions that enable or limit armored mobility.

  • ISR Operations

    Separate intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance, then judge whether an ISR product answers the commander's question in time.

Module 3 · Battalion/Brigade Operations Planning

  • Troop Leading Procedure (TLP)

    Use the eight Troop Leading Procedure steps to organize preparation time, inform subordinates early, and refine the plan as facts change.

Module 4 · Signal Support Integration

  • Signal Support in Combined Arms Operations

    Signal support connects the Army's combined arms team so commanders can direct activity and participating units can coordinate across preparation, movement and operations at the destination.

  • Signal Support in Joint Operations

    Joint signal support connects the Philippine Army, Navy and Air Force so commanders, operating units and supporting elements can coordinate despite differences in equipment and procedures.

  • Signal Support in Coalition Operations

    Coalition signal support lets forces from different nations exchange the information they need for a common mission while honoring each participant's sharing and protection requirements.

Module 5 · Advanced C2 Communications

  • MANET C4ISTAR Planning

    A mobile ad hoc network (MANET) lets mobile nodes form and adapt wireless paths without a permanent infrastructure. Plan the services those paths must carry and verify that each service works under exercise conditions.

  • C4ISTAR Sensors Integration

    C4ISTAR sensor integration makes observations available and understandable to the people who need them. It supports a shared picture through usable data, compatible interfaces and clear responsibilities.

  • C4ISTAR Weapon System Integration

    C4ISTAR weapon system integration links information and decision support across sensors, networks and command elements. The lesson separates information exchange from command authority and assesses the exchange in a simulated setting.

  • Signal Propagation (Mobile Apps/RF Clouds)

    Propagation models estimate radio coverage and link performance. Compare the modeled results on consistent assumptions, then verify the links and gaps that matter to the exercise.

Module 6 · Advanced Electronic Warfare Operations

  • Signals Intelligence (SIGINT)

    Signals intelligence turns relevant observations from communications and non-communications signals into assessed information for a decision. Evaluate the quality of a report and share it with authorized recipients who need it.

  • Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)

    Electronic intelligence (ELINT) interprets non-communication emissions such as radar signals. Distinguish the features you observe, what they suggest about the emitter, and what evidence would support a firmer assessment.

  • Radio Direction Finding

    Radio direction finding estimates the direction from which an RF signal arrives. Use that bearing to support a decision while preserving the difference between a direction and a position.

  • Electronic Counter-Countermeasures (ECCM)

    Electronic counter-countermeasures are taught as Electronic Protection (EP). Use communications discipline and continuity planning to preserve friendly service, then distinguish an observed disruption from its possible causes.

Module 7 · Advanced Cyberspace Operations

  • NIST Framework

    Use the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 to identify desired cybersecurity outcomes, assess gaps and prioritize work by mission risk.

  • Zero Trust

    Zero trust evaluates each session against resource-specific policy using verified identity, device state and other relevant context.

  • LOE3 GSOMIA Compliance

    Apply the lesson’s information-protection principles to controlled sharing of sensitive military information with authorized partners.

  • Cyber Risk Assessment

    Assess defensive cyber risks by linking a valued asset, a credible threat and a weakness to likelihood and mission impact.

Module 8 · Signal Planning

  • CEOI / CESI Management

    Distinguish changing system-specific operating instructions from standing instructions, then verify that a classroom copy is authoritative, effective and complete.

  • Signal Estimate

    A signal estimate organizes known and assumed factors, compares courses of action and recommends how to meet signal support requirements.

  • C4ISTAR Communications Planning

    Plan communications around the information that C4ISTAR components must exchange, the decisions that depend on it and the conditions in which it must arrive.

  • Signal Planning Process

    Develop signal support requirements, allocate feasible resources, prepare for disruptions and validate the plan before publication.

Supporting Lessons · Supporting study material; not separately numbered POI lessons

  • Army Operations

    Distinguish the platforms, operation types, tenets and principles used in the Philippine Army lesson, then apply them to a classroom coordination decision.

  • Intelligence Cycle

    Follow the five stages of the intelligence cycle to turn collected information into a decision-support assessment and revise it as needs or evidence change.
