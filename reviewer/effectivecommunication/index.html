01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Effective communication requires a suitable method and channel plus feedback that verifies the receiver's meaning.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Process and facilities

Recall it, then reveal

Communication exchanges information, ideas, and attitudes through shared signs, symbols, or behavior; communications refers to the facilities that carry the exchange.

Why it matters A working facility establishes delivery capability, while effective meaning still requires checking.

Lesson source: page 22 · page 23 · page 33 · page 36

02

Four methods

Recall it, then reveal

The methods are spoken, nonverbal, written, and visual; tone, posture, and facial expression can reinforce or undermine spoken words.

Why it matters A message may use several methods at once.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 25 · page 26 · page 27 · page 28 · page 34

03

Direction and tool choice

Recall it, then reveal

Downward messages go to subordinates, upward messages to leaders, and lateral messages among peers; video discussion supports remote rapport, messaging supports quick updates, and tracking tools align goals and timelines.

Why it matters Direction describes the organizational relationship, while the tool serves the communication task.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 20 · page 27 · page 29 · page 33 · page 34 · page 35

04

Feedback loop

Recall it, then reveal

The linear model asks who says what, through which channel, to whom, and with what effect; two-way communication adds feedback and exchanges sender/receiver roles.

Why it matters A specific restatement of task, place, or time tests interpretation.

Lesson source: page 37 · page 38 · page 39 · page 41 · page 42

05

Five barrier categories

Recall it, then reveal

The barrier categories are language, psychological, physiological, physical, and attitudinal.

Why it matters Match the remedy to observed evidence: unfamiliar jargon calls for clear language, while misunderstood concerns call for listening and clarification.

Lesson source: page 48 · page 49 · page 50 · page 51 · page 52 · page 53

06

Practical techniques

Recall it, then reveal

Listen actively without interrupting, put the purpose first, use concise familiar language, and focus constructive feedback on the situation or performance.

Why it matters These behaviors expose meaning gaps and make an improvement response possible.

05Know the facts

Communication process
A sender conveys a message through a channel to a receiver. Feedback gives the receiver a way to respond and clarify meaning.
Four methods
Spoken, nonverbal, written, and visual. Tone and gestures can reinforce or complicate the meaning of spoken words.
Organizational direction
Messages can move downward to subordinates, upward to leaders, or horizontally among peers.
Barriers
Language, psychological, physiological, physical, and attitudinal barriers can interfere with receiving or interpreting a message.
Practical techniques
Use active listening, clear and concise language, emotional awareness, and constructive feedback focused on the issue.

06Build your understanding

Communication, communications, and purpose

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, and attitudes through a common system of signs, symbols, or behavior and a medium. The lesson uses communications for the facilities that carry the exchange. Effective communication builds relationships, supports teams and personnel management, enables innovation, and improves transparency. The ability to deliver a message and the ability of a receiver to understand it are separate checks.

  • Facilities carry the message; shared meaning is the communication goal.
  • Start with the information the receiver must understand or use.

Watch out: Diagnosing every misunderstanding as a fault in the communications system.

Lesson source: page 22 · page 23 · page 33 · page 36

Methods, directions, and tools

The four methods are spoken, nonverbal, written, and visual. Direction describes the organizational path: downward to subordinates, upward to leaders, and lateral between peers or equivalent teams. Tool choice depends on the task. The lesson uses video conferencing for remote discussion and rapport, instant messaging for quick updates, and project tracking for aligning goals and timelines.

  • Method describes how the message is expressed; direction describes who sends it to whom.
  • Use a map or graphic for spatial meaning and a written record for details that must be retained.

Watch out: Calling a memo downward communication solely because it is written.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 20 · page 25 · page 26 · page 27 · page 28 · page 29 · page 33 · page 34 · page 35

The message process and feedback

The linear model asks who says what, through which channel, to whom, and with what effect. Two-way communication adds feedback and allows sender and receiver to exchange roles. Feedback can reveal whether the receiver interpreted the task, priorities, or timing correctly. Ask for a specific response that lets you compare the intended message with the received meaning.

  • Recall sender, message, channel, receiver, and feedback.
  • Use a check-back when shared interpretation matters.

Watch out: Taking a receipt acknowledgment as proof the receiver can act on the instruction.

Lesson source: page 37 · page 38 · page 39 · page 41 · page 42

Barriers and their remedies

Barriers prevent reception or understanding. The lesson groups barriers as language, psychological, physiological, physical, and attitudinal and lists examples including jargon, emotion, distraction, perception, hearing or speech limitations, and language or cultural differences. Diagnose the barrier from the facts before choosing a remedy. Simple language helps with jargon, active listening helps clarify meaning, and constructive feedback addresses a specific issue.

  • Identify the symptom and its likely cause separately.
  • Match the remedy to what prevented understanding.

Watch out: Repeating technical terms more loudly when the audience does not understand the terms.

Lesson source: page 48 · page 49 · page 50 · page 51 · page 52 · page 53

Listening, clarity, and feedback in practice

Active listening means attending to the speaker, avoiding interruption, and trying to understand the concern before replying. Clarity and conciseness put the purpose first and remove unnecessary jargon. Tone, facial expression, and posture can reinforce or undermine the words. Constructive feedback focuses on the situation or performance and invites a response that helps improve understanding.

  • Lead with the main purpose and keep the language direct.
  • Attend to words, tone, and nonverbal cues.

Watch out: Offering criticism about the person when a specific behavior or condition is the issue.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 51 · page 52 · page 53 · page 54

07Understand the reasoning

  • An immediate verbal update is fast, while a written or visual aid gives people a reference they can recheck after the conversation.
  • A receipt acknowledgement confirms delivery. A receiver who restates the action and deadline provides evidence of understanding.
  • When a message is delivered successfully yet the team acts incorrectly, check the interpretation and the usefulness of the feedback.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A Philippine Army training coordinator announces a room change aloud. Two section leaders hear the new room number, but one assumes the change also delays the start.

How should the coordinator correct the misunderstanding and verify the revised instructions?

Show the decision and reasoning

State the room and start time together and update the shared schedule. Ask each section leader to restate where and when the group must report. Spoken clarification reaches the group quickly and the written schedule gives everyone the same reference.

09How to practice

  1. State the required action, timing, and any important change in plain language.
  2. Choose a channel that reaches the audience and provide a reference when details must be retained.
  3. Ask the receiver to restate the action in their own words.
  4. Correct the specific misunderstanding and confirm the final shared version.

10Check yourself

What role does feedback play in two-way communication?

It lets the receiver respond and lets the sender verify or clarify what was understood.

What type of barrier is unfamiliar jargon when the words can be heard clearly?

A language barrier.
