01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Classify Army operations by purpose and force composition separately, then use tenets, warfighting functions and principles to explain the decision and its tradeoffs.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Mandate and platforms

Recall it, then reveal

The lesson cites the AFP role of protecting the people and State and securing sovereignty and territorial integrity; joint operations involve two or more services under one commander, while combined arms integrates multiple Army arms.

Why it matters Force composition and command relationship identify the platform rather than the civil or combat purpose.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 10

02

Three operation elements

Recall it, then reveal

Full spectrum operations comprise offensive operations, defensive operations and support to civil governance.

Why it matters A disaster-support purpose and participation by multiple services can describe the same operation on different dimensions.

Lesson source: page 11 · page 12 · page 13 · page 14

03

Offensive and defensive tasks

Recall it, then reveal

Offensive examples include movement to contact, attack, exploitation, strike and pursuit; defensive examples include mobile defense, area defense and retrograde.

Why it matters Classify the assigned task by its purpose; retaining initiative can still matter during defense.

Lesson source: page 11 · page 12 · page 13

04

Five tenets

Recall it, then reveal

Initiative shapes events; agility responds quickly; depth extends effort in space, time, purpose and resources; synchronization aligns effects in time and space; versatility meets varied requirements.

Why it matters Speed by itself does not prove that separate activities will combine at the required place and time.

Lesson source: page 15 · page 16 · page 17 · page 18 · page 19 · page 20

05

Eight warfighting functions

Recall it, then reveal

Movement and maneuver, intelligence, fires, civil military operations, information operation, sustainment, command and control, and protection; leadership ties them together and information enhances them.

Why it matters Classify each task by its primary contribution rather than by the fact it uses a report or a communications system.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 22 · page 23 · page 24 · page 25 · page 26 · page 27 · page 28 · page 29 · page 30 · page 31 · page 32 · page 33

06

Twelve principles

Recall it, then reveal

Objective, offensive, mass, economy of force, maneuver, unity of command, security, surprise, simplicity, perseverance, legitimacy and restraint guide operational choices.

Why it matters Concentrating effort at the decisive place and preserving minimum essential support elsewhere illustrate complementary allocation principles.

02Don’t confuse these

Joint

Combined arms

Joint uses two or more services under a single commander; combined arms integrates several Army arms. Multiple Army arms alone do not establish a joint operation.

Lesson source: page 10

Agility

/

Synchronization

Agility concerns quick response; synchronization arranges interdependent effects in time and space. Fast arrival can still be poorly synchronized.

Economy of force

05Know the facts

Constitutional mandate
The lesson cites Article II of the 1987 Constitution. The AFP protects the people and the State, with the goal of securing State sovereignty and national territorial integrity.
Operational platforms
Multinational or combined operations involve an alliance or coalition. Joint operations involve forces from two or more services under a single commander. Combined arms integrates several Army arms.
Full spectrum operations
The lesson groups these into offensive operations, defensive operations and support to civil governance.
Support to civil governance
Lesson examples include disaster response, civil population and vital installation security, and support to community development.
Five tenets
Initiative, agility, depth, synchronization and versatility describe qualities of effective Army operations.
Army power and principles
The lesson presents Army power through warfighting functions tied together by leadership. Principles such as objective, unity of command and simplicity help assess operational choices.

Topic 1 of 6

Mandate and organizational platforms

The lesson cites Article II of the 1987 Constitution for the AFP’s role protecting the people and the State and securing sovereignty and territorial integrity. It distinguishes coalition or alliance operations, joint operations by two or more services under one commander, and combined arms operations integrating multiple Army arms.

  • Classify a platform by the composition and command relationship of the forces.

Watch out: Multiple Army arms alone do not make an operation joint.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 10

Topic 2 of 6

Offensive and defensive tasks

The lesson groups operations into offensive, defensive and support to civil governance. Offensive operations aim to defeat opposing forces and seize objectives, with tasks such as movement to contact, attack, exploitation, strike and pursuit. Defensive tasks include mobile defense, area defense and retrograde, and serve purposes such as defeating attacks or gaining time.

  • Classify the stated task and explain its purpose in the scenario.

Watch out: The offensive principle of retaining initiative can still apply in a defensive situation.

Lesson source: page 11 · page 12 · page 13

Topic 3 of 6

Support to civil governance

The lesson places disaster response, protection of civil populations and installations, support to law enforcement and community development within this element. The activities involve coordination with other instruments of national power to maintain or restore a safe environment. Identify the civil objective and the military support requested.

  • Distinguish what the operation is doing from who is participating.

Watch out: An operation can support civil governance while also being joint.

Lesson source: page 11 · page 14

Topic 4 of 6

Five tenets

Initiative shapes events, agility responds quickly, depth extends an effort in space, time, purpose and resources, synchronization coordinates effects in time and space, and versatility meets varied requirements. A classroom case can show several tenets. Explain which behavior is most directly evidenced.

  • Look for the feature the scenario actually describes.

Watch out: Speed alone does not demonstrate that activities are synchronized.

Lesson source: page 15 · page 16 · page 17 · page 18 · page 19 · page 20

Topic 5 of 6

Army power and its eight functions

Leadership ties the functions together, and information enhances them. The lesson lists movement and maneuver, intelligence, fires, civil military operations, information operation, sustainment, command and control, and protection. Distinguish producing understanding, directing activity, supporting endurance and preserving the force.

  • Classify a task by its primary contribution.

Watch out: A report can assist C2 while the analysis producing it belongs to intelligence.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 22 · page 23 · page 24 · page 25 · page 26 · page 27 · page 28 · page 29 · page 30 · page 31 · page 32 · page 33

Topic 6 of 6

Twelve principles and their tradeoffs

The lesson lists objective, offensive, mass, economy of force, maneuver, unity of command, security, surprise, simplicity, perseverance, legitimacy and restraint. Consider how principles work together. Focusing effort at the decisive place uses mass, while assigning the minimum essential resources to secondary efforts uses economy of force. Clear orders support simplicity.

  • Explain why the stated decision fits the principle and what it costs.

Watch out: Assigning every resource to one place can leave essential secondary tasks unsupported.

Lesson source: page 35 · page 36 · page 37 · page 38 · page 39 · page 40 · page 41 · page 42 · page 43 · page 44 · page 45 · page 46 · page 47

07Understand the reasoning

  • The operation type describes the activity’s purpose. The platform describes how the participating forces are organized. A disaster-response activity can also involve more than one service.
  • Synchronization makes interdependent activities work in time and space. In a relief exercise, a shared handoff time and location can prevent supplies from arriving before the receiving team is ready.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A fictional Philippine Army classroom team supports a municipal disaster drill. Transport can bring training supplies to a shelter before the receiving team is ready, or wait for the shelter’s confirmed intake window.

Which course better applies synchronization, and what is the tradeoff?

Show the decision and reasoning

Use the confirmed intake window and communicate the handoff to both teams. Arrival may be later, but the supplies are more likely to be accepted and distributed immediately. Keep the coordination simple by naming the handoff lead and the update that triggers a change.

10Objective & analytical practice

Answer first, then reveal the explanation. Situations are fictional classroom exercises applying the lesson.

objectiveWhat distinguishes joint operations from combined arms operations?

Answer: Joint involves two or more services under a single commander. Combined arms integrates several Army arms.

The first distinction is by service and command, the second by Army capabilities working together.

Lesson source: page 10

objectiveWhat ties the eight warfighting functions together, and what enhances them?

Answer: Leadership ties them together and information enhances them.

The lesson treats both as integral to the force’s combined Army power.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 22 · page 25

analyticalAn Army team and another service support a municipal evacuation under one commander. What are the operation type and platform in this tabletop?

Answer: Support to civil governance and joint, respectively.

Disaster support identifies the purpose. Participation of two services under one commander identifies the platform.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 14

analyticalA simulated relief team drives quickly, but no shelter staff are present when it arrives. What tenet most directly addresses the problem?

Answer: Synchronization.

The issue is the alignment of the transport and receiving teams in time and space.

Lesson source: page 17 · page 19

analyticalA classroom plan concentrates support at the decisive point and assigns the minimum needed to maintain another site. Which two principles explain the allocation?

Answer: Mass and economy of force.

The plan focuses effects and provides an essential minimum for the secondary effort.

Lesson source: page 38 · page 39
