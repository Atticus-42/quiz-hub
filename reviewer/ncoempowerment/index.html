01Quick briefing

Distinguish NCO duties, responsibility, and authority, then match an inspection or correction to the actual training need.

Read with NCO Empowerment.

02Know the facts

Taking charge
Clarify expectations and reporting relationships, assess team competence, and align the team's work with the higher mission.
Three types of duties
Specified duties belong to the position, directed duties are assigned, and implied duties arise from what is needed to carry out a responsibility.
Responsibility and authority
Responsibility is accountability for performance and standards. Command authority follows rank or assignment, while general military authority applies as prescribed across the service.
Inspection and correction
An inspection checks compliance or readiness. A correction addresses an observed deficiency, with corrective training tied to the performance gap.
NCO support channel
Supports the chain of command by advising leaders and helping implement training, standards, and enlisted readiness within command policy.

03Build your understanding

Taking charge as an NCO

An NCO taking a new position learns the unit and higher mission, expected standards, available resources, current morale, and the capabilities of direct reports. Learn what the immediate leader expects and who outside the unit can support the work. That assessment helps set priorities for small-unit training, team building, and readiness within established policy.

  • Start with mission, standards, people, and resources.
  • Link the unit's work to the mission of the next higher organization.

Watch out: Changing routines before checking how the unit performs and what support is available.

Duties, responsibilities, and authority

A duty is a task or assignment. Specified duties are tied to the position, directed duties come from instruction outside the normal position description, and implied duties are supporting work that follows from the mission or role. Responsibility is the wider obligation and accountability for performance and conduct. Authority is the legitimate power to make and enforce decisions within the military hierarchy.

  • Classify a duty by how it arises.
  • Distinguish command responsibility for unit results from each soldier's individual responsibility.

Watch out: Treating a duty assignment as a grant of unrestricted authority.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 22 · page 23 · page 24 · page 28 · page 29

Command and general military authority

Command authority comes through rank or assignment and provides authority over soldiers in the relevant relationship. The lesson traces it to the President, with law and regulation able to supplement it. General military authority allows soldiers to act when the unit leader or other designated authority is absent. In a case, establish whether the actor is exercising assigned command or acting in the absence of the designated leader.

  • Command authority depends on the relevant rank or assignment.
  • General military authority supports action in the absence of the designated authority.

Watch out: Using general military authority as a basis to assume the full powers of a unit commander.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29

Inspections and readiness

Inspection establishes condition or compliance. In-ranks inspection concerns personnel and equipment in formation. In-quarters inspection includes appearance, weapons, equipment, maintenance, and sanitary conditions. An informal unscheduled inspection checks a current condition. Pre-combat and pre-execution checks and inspections test readiness for a planned activity. A correction addresses a deficiency already found.

  • Use the purpose and timing to classify the check.
  • Separate observing the deficiency from the action that corrects it.

Watch out: Calling an unscheduled check a correction simply because it later uncovers a deficiency.

Lesson source: page 30 · page 31 · page 32 · page 35

Correct the performance

On-the-spot correction addresses a specific performance deficiency, one correction at a time, and ends when the correction is complete. Corrective training must relate directly to the deficiency and continue only until the deficiency is overcome. The goal is improved performance and accountability. If further action is needed, keep the applicable disciplinary safeguards in place.

  • Address the performance with a task-linked correction.
  • Verify improvement and end the corrective training when the gap is overcome.

Watch out: Using unrelated, prolonged activity as corrective training.

Lesson source: page 31 · page 33 · page 34 · page 36

The NCO Support Channel

The support channel links senior and junior NCOs to communicate standards, run daily operations within policy, train and develop soldiers, account for equipment, and support soldiers and families. It advises and assists the formal chain of command, including on enlisted rewards and punishment. A recurring readiness problem can be addressed through NCO coordination while issues requiring a command decision go to the proper authority.

  • The support channel complements the chain of command.
  • Connect readiness, welfare, training, and accountability to the NCO role.

Watch out: Treating the support channel as a separate authority that can override the chain.

04Understand the reasoning

  • An empowered NCO understands both the standard to enforce and the authority available to act. Clarifying expectations early prevents conflicting directions.
  • A focused correction fits an isolated performance gap. A wider readiness check makes sense when the same problem may affect several teams.
  • During a scenario, identify the evidence of a deficiency before choosing the correction and the authority to act.

05Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A Philippine Army platoon sergeant notices that one team can explain the approved equipment handover but repeatedly leaves items off the checklist when demonstrating it.

Should the NCO correct the observed performance or immediately order a platoon-wide inspection?

Show the decision and reasoning

Coach the team on the omitted check and have it demonstrate a complete handover. Ask other team leaders to confirm they use the same standard if there are signs the error is shared. The focused correction addresses the immediate gap, while a broader inspection would help determine the extent of a suspected common problem.

06How to practice

  1. Confirm the applicable standard and the authority for the correction.
  2. Describe the observed deficiency and its effect on readiness.
  3. Demonstrate the expected action and have the soldier repeat it.
  4. Check performance against the same standard and raise recurring or wider problems through the appropriate leadership channel.

Review the steps with NCO Empowerment.

07Check yourself

What distinguishes a directed duty from an implied duty?

A directed duty is assigned. An implied duty follows from what is necessary to carry out a responsibility.

What purpose does the NCO support channel serve?

It supports the chain of command through advice and execution of training, standards, and enlisted readiness.

08Objective & analytical practice

Answer first, then reveal the explanation. Situations are fictional classroom exercises applying the lesson.

objectiveWhat are the three types of duty?

Answer: Specified, directed, and implied.

They differ by whether the task follows the position, an additional direction, or supporting work implied by the role.

Lesson source: page 22

analyticalA new platoon sergeant finds morale low and cannot tell which training gaps affect the higher mission. What should the sergeant establish first?

Answer: Confirm the mission, higher-unit priorities, standards, resources, and current performance with leaders and soldiers.

Those facts give the new NCO a basis for goals and for deciding which gaps to address first.

Lesson source: page 15 · page 16 · page 17

objectiveHow does individual responsibility differ from general military authority?

Answer: Individual responsibility is the soldier's obligation to perform and improve. General military authority permits action in the absence of the designated leader or authority.

One describes accountability for conduct; the other describes a basis to act.

Lesson source: page 23 · page 24 · page 29

analyticalAn NCO makes an informal unscheduled check and finds a recurring equipment preparation deficiency. How should the two parts of the response be classified?

Answer: The check is an on-the-spot inspection. The immediate instruction or retraining is a correction.

Detection and remedy serve different purposes even when they happen close together.

Lesson source: page 30 · page 31 · page 35

analyticalA soldier omits one required handover entry. How should corrective training be designed?

Answer: Show the missing step, have the soldier practice that entry in the handover process, and stop once the deficiency is overcome.

The training focuses on the documented performance gap and has a measurable stopping point.

objectiveWhat relationship does the NCO Support Channel have to the formal chain of command?

Answer: It complements and assists the chain of command.

It helps implement standards and communicate enlisted concerns while advising the commander where required.

09Before the exam

  • Distinguish duty, responsibility, and authority before classifying a case.
  • Recognize inspections by purpose and choose corrections tied directly to the deficiency.
  • Explain how the support channel moves training, standards, and soldier concerns through the unit.
