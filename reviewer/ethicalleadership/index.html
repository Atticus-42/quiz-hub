01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Ethical leadership protects trustworthy decisions and readiness by aligning conduct with values, testing options, and correcting both failures and misleading reports.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Values and readiness

Recall it, then reveal

Honor, patriotism, and duty guide ethical conduct; lawful, ethical action supports legitimacy, while accurate reporting exposes readiness vulnerabilities.

Why it matters False readiness claims can distort higher headquarters' plans.

Lesson source: page 6 · page 7 · page 8

02

Six-step decision process

Recall it, then reveal

Define the dilemma; identify applicable laws, orders, regulations, and values; generate feasible options; evaluate legal, moral, and mission implications; decide and take responsibility; reflect on the outcome.

Why it matters Explicit comparison tests alternatives before action and supports learning afterward.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 12 · page 13

03

Three ethical lenses

Recall it, then reveal

Rules-based reasoning tests duties and constraints; outcomes-based reasoning weighs consequences; virtue-based reasoning tests character and Army values.

Why it matters The same option may appear attractive through one lens but reveal a serious issue through another.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 11 · page 12

04

Integrity and reporting

Recall it, then reveal

Integrity keeps values, words, and deeds consistent, including disclosing and correcting discrepancies through the chain when reporting carries personal cost.

Why it matters Fixing equipment or completing a missed check does not by itself correct an already false report.

Lesson source: page 8 · page 12 · page 15 · page 16 · page 17

05

Transactional baseline

Recall it, then reveal

Contingent reward recognizes expected performance; active management-by-exception monitors and corrects deviations; passive management-by-exception intervenes after problems arise.

Why it matters Clear expectations and correction sustain standards.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 21 · page 22

06

Four I's and augmentation

Recall it, then reveal

Idealized influence models standards; inspirational motivation gives shared purpose; intellectual stimulation challenges assumptions; individualized consideration tailors development. Transformational leadership adds these to a transactional baseline.

Why it matters The augmentation effect combines standards with commitment to improve beyond them.

05Know the facts

Ethical leadership and integrity
Base choices on the Army Ethic and Values and keep actions consistent with stated standards, especially under pressure.
Three ethical lenses
Rules-based reasoning checks duties and constraints. Outcomes-based reasoning weighs effects. Virtue-based reasoning asks what conduct reflects Army Values and character.
Operational legitimacy
Lawful and ethical conduct supports confidence in the unit and the legitimacy of its actions.
Transactional leadership
Uses clear expectations, contingent rewards, and corrective action to manage performance.
The Four I's
Idealized influence models standards. Inspirational motivation gives shared purpose. Intellectual stimulation encourages better ideas. Individualized consideration tailors development.

06Build your understanding

Army Values, legitimacy, and readiness

Ethical leadership aligns decisions and conduct with the Army Ethic and the Philippine Army values of honor, patriotism, and duty. Ethical and legal conduct supports mission legitimacy. Consistent conduct earns the trust that holds a force together under pressure. Accurate reporting keeps hidden vulnerabilities from becoming operational surprises. In a Signal case, pressure to report readiness raises the value of checking and reporting the actual status.

  • Link ethical conduct to legitimacy, cohesion, and readiness.
  • Evaluate decisions under time pressure and when conduct is unobserved.

Watch out: Measuring success solely by the time shown on a readiness report.

Lesson source: page 6 · page 7 · page 8

Six steps for an ethical decision

Define the dilemma and identify the duties or values in tension. Identify the laws, orders, regulations, and values that apply. Generate feasible courses of action and test each for legal, moral, and mission implications. Decide and take responsibility for the action. Reflect on the outcome so the unit can learn and prevent recurrence. The process works best when alternatives are compared explicitly before action.

  • Define, know rules, develop options, evaluate, decide and act, reflect.
  • Keep the problem and candidate courses of action separate.

Watch out: Settling on a convenient action first and using evaluation to justify it afterward.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 12 · page 13

Rules, outcomes, and virtue

A rules-based lens asks whether an option fits applicable laws, orders, and regulations. An outcomes-based lens weighs consequences for mission accomplishment, soldiers, and the unit. A virtue-based lens asks what conduct expresses strong character and Army values. Apply all three to the same options to expose tensions, such as a quick local fix that leaves higher headquarters relying on false information.

  • Rules set boundaries; outcomes compare effects; virtue examines character.
  • A feasible correction should address both the actual problem and information already submitted.

Watch out: Treating a favorable immediate outcome as sufficient justification for violating a required standard.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 11 · page 12

Integrity and the reporting chain

Integrity is consistency between values, words, and deeds, including when accurate reporting has a personal cost. A concealed inventory discrepancy or false compliance report undermines the information on which others base decisions. The lesson's COMSEC case demonstrates reporting and correcting through the chain. Its inventory case also calls attention to the signal leaders send when they disclose and fix discrepancies.

  • Report known facts accurately and initiate correction.
  • Connect small routine decisions with the long-term climate of trust.

Watch out: Quietly fixing the physical issue while leaving a false official report in place.

Lesson source: page 12 · page 15 · page 16 · page 17

Transactional leadership and augmentation

Transactional leadership sets expectations and uses recognition or correction to sustain standards. Contingent reward recognizes expected performance. Active management-by-exception monitors deviations and corrects them, while passive management-by-exception intervenes after problems arise. Transformational leadership adds shared purpose, initiative, and growth. The augmentation effect combines a clear baseline with commitment to improve beyond it.

  • Standards and monitoring provide a dependable foundation.
  • Shared purpose and development can extend performance beyond the baseline.

Watch out: Dropping required checks because a team appears motivated and committed.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 21 · page 22 · page 27

The Four I's in unit practice

Idealized influence makes the leader's example worth following. Inspirational motivation explains a compelling vision and shared purpose. Intellectual stimulation invites people to question assumptions and improve methods. Individualized consideration develops people through coaching and support tailored to their needs. One episode can show several mechanisms, so choose the one indicated by the particular action in the question.

  • Example, shared purpose, innovation, and tailored development are different signals.
  • A commander can model a standard while inviting improvements to the way the team meets it.

Watch out: Labeling every positive effect as inspirational motivation even when the decisive action was tailored coaching.

Lesson source: page 20 · page 24 · page 25 · page 26

07Understand the reasoning

  • Check applicable rules before weighing convenience. A choice can save time and still produce a misleading report or violate a duty.
  • Correcting one lapse sets a standard. Modeling candor and inviting the team to improve its checking process can also change what people do under the next deadline.
  • When an ethical case involves a report, analyze the condition on the ground and the information already in the chain as separate issues.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

Before a Philippine Army class demonstration, a team leader learns that a required equipment check was skipped. The group has already described its preparations as complete, and the schedule has little slack.

How should the leader act now, and how could the leader reduce pressure to conceal the next problem?

Show the decision and reasoning

Update the instructor with the actual status and complete the check before using the equipment. If needed, present an approved substitute or revise the demonstration schedule. Own the reporting error openly and ask the group to redesign how it records completed checks. Accuracy protects the next decision, and the follow-up gives the team a way to improve.

09How to practice

  1. Define the dilemma and identify the duties or values in tension.
  2. Identify applicable rules, orders, and Army Values.
  3. Develop feasible courses of action.
  4. Evaluate each option through rules, outcomes, and virtues.
  5. Choose, act, and take responsibility for the decision.
  6. Reflect on the outcome and identify a lesson for future decisions.
