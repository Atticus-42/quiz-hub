01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

A MANET adapts among mobile nodes, but continuity and integration must be proved for each required service.

04Quick briefing

A mobile ad hoc network (MANET) lets mobile nodes form and adapt wireless paths without a permanent infrastructure. Plan the services those paths must carry and verify that each service works under exercise conditions.

Read with MANET C4ISTAR Planning.

05Know the facts

Mobile nodes
Nodes can forward traffic for one another across multiple hops where a usable path exists.
Self-healing
The network can adapt to a topology change by using an available alternate path. A disconnected group may still lose service.
Traffic and capacity
Unicast serves an individual destination, while broadcast reaches a broader audience. Quality of service helps manage competing traffic within available capacity.
Diagnostics
Received signal strength and signal-to-noise ratio measure different conditions. Review them alongside service and network evidence.
Fault handling
Identify and isolate the fault, carry out the approved repair and verify that the affected service has recovered.
Integration and protection
An integrator can connect legacy and newer services. Verify each needed service separately and preserve the controls on what may cross the boundary.

06Build your understanding

Mobile topology and recovery

A mobile ad hoc network forms among mobile nodes without fixed infrastructure. Connectivity can change as nodes move, disappear, or rejoin. The lesson contrasts recovery by rebuilding a route with a flooding example in which remaining paths carry the traffic. Self-healing depends on viable connectivity among the remaining nodes.

  • Self-forming establishes the network and self-healing adapts to change.
  • Unicast, multicast, and broadcast describe different recipients.

Watch out: Self-healing cannot make an isolated node reachable on its own.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 11 · page 12 · page 13 · page 14 · page 15 · page 17 · page 18 · page 19

Services, waveforms and protection

The E-LynX examples distinguish narrowband, wideband, high-capacity mobile ad hoc, and compatibility waveforms. Match the supported services to the task, including voice, data, and position reports. Quality of service matters when different traffic shares a constrained network. Legacy or NATO compatibility still requires a separate check that the service and protection meet the mission need.

  • The chosen waveform affects both services and interoperability.
  • A waveform's compatibility and encryption properties are distinct.

Watch out: Assuming every voice mode carries secure data can leave a planned exchange unavailable.

Lesson source: page 12 · page 28 · page 30 · page 31 · page 32 · page 33 · page 34 · page 35 · page 36

Maintenance and restoration evidence

The lesson uses the handheld and vehicular adapter to show that different equipment packages have different support needs. Preventive maintenance uses inspection and cleaning to preserve equipment. Corrective work follows identification, isolation, repair, and verification. Reproducing the affected function after repair gives the user evidence that it is restored.

  • Isolation narrows the problem before replacement or repair.
  • Verification follows repair and may include built-in testing.

Watch out: A radio that powers up after maintenance may still fail to provide the service that prompted the repair.

Lesson source: page 23 · page 24 · page 25 · page 26 · page 27 · page 38 · page 46 · page 47

Interpret diagnostic evidence

Node information helps the technician identify the local and remote radios. Received signal strength indicates level, while signal-to-noise ratio describes received quality relative to noise. Ping and packet tests offer different evidence about connectivity and performance. Use evidence from the affected path and service instead of assuming that one diagnostic proves the entire network works.

  • Associate readings with the correct node and path.
  • Separate signal level, signal quality, reachability, and service performance.

Watch out: Good received strength alone does not explain missing packets or broken voice.

Lesson source: page 54 · page 55 · page 56 · page 58 · page 60 · page 66 · page 67

Integration across field, command post and headquarters

The course architecture shows field, command post, headquarters, and legacy elements connected through an integrator. Position, video, data, and voice have distinct paths. Traffic separation supports security, limits traffic leakage, and reduces unnecessary traffic. Evaluate each user service from sender to recipient, since legacy voice and IP-based data can behave differently.

  • Follow each required service through its own interfaces.
  • A passing voice exchange provides evidence for the voice path.

Watch out: Accepting the whole system from one passing service can conceal a separate data failure.

Lesson source: page 22 · page 69 · page 70 · page 71 · page 72 · page 73 · page 74 · page 75 · page 76

07Understand the reasoning

  • A network can reroute and still struggle with heavy updates. Judge restoration using the service the user needs, including its delay or loss.
  • A connected node and a successful voice test give limited evidence about data exchange. Each required service has its own traffic and compatibility demands.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

During a Philippine Army classroom simulation, one moving node loses its usual path. The network reroutes traffic and voice works, but map updates arrive too late for the exercise's movement decision.

What should the team verify before it declares the network restored?

Show the decision and reasoning

Confirm that the receiving team gets a usable map update within the exercise's decision window. Compare the data path and traffic behavior before and after the reroute, and check whether the delay affects only that service. If capacity is limited, prioritize essential exchanges and repeat the same test.

09How to practice

  1. Define a success check for each required classroom service, such as intelligible voice or a timely map update.
  2. Record which simulated nodes and services work before the instructor introduces a path or interface fault.
  3. Use the authorized diagnostic view to compare the affected path and services, then isolate the likely fault.
  4. Repeat the original service checks after the instructor-approved correction and record the outcome.

10Check yourself

What must exist for self-healing to restore connectivity after a path breaks?

A usable alternate path between the affected nodes.

Why should repair verification go beyond seeing that a node is connected?

Connectivity alone leaves the actual delivery and performance of the required service unverified.
