01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Field artillery delivers timely, accurate support through an integrated system whose role, desired effect, and weapon classification answer different questions.

Mission

Three tactical roles

06Build your understanding

Field artillery mission and functions

Field artillery supports ground forces with timely and accurate fires to destroy, neutralize, or suppress the enemy and assists the integration of all fires in combined arms operations. Its functions include providing continuous fires and furnishing the communications, mobility, survey, meteorological information, target acquisition, and fire direction that make the support usable. The maneuver commander's requirements establish what support must achieve.

  • The purpose is support to the ground force and the combined arms plan.
  • Timeliness, accuracy, and continuity describe different qualities of the support.

Watch out: Assuming that weapon availability alone demonstrates responsive support.

Lesson source: page 15 · page 16

Tactical roles and desired effects

Close support engages forces or positions threatening the supported force. Attack at depth, or interdiction, disrupts, delays, or destroys enemy forces beyond the immediate fight and can open maneuver opportunities. Counterfire attacks the enemy fire support system. The desired effect is a separate decision: destroy renders a force combat-ineffective, neutralize prevents interference with a particular operation, and suppress temporarily degrades performance below what the enemy needs to accomplish its mission.

  • Classify the tactical role by the problem addressed and the desired effect by the required result.
  • Disrupt upsets formation or tempo, defeat involves loss of means or will to fight, and diversion draws attention or forces away from the main operation.

Watch out: Choosing destruction whenever a brief, defined period of reduced enemy performance would meet the stated purpose.

Lesson source: page 17 · page 25 · page 26 · page 27 · page 28

Capabilities, limitations, and the Philippine environment

The lesson identifies the ability to shift and mass fires quickly without displacing, use varied shell or fuze combinations, and sustain continuous support through judicious displacement. Range limits, firing signatures, limited self-defense, and the ammunition demand for point targets constrain employment. In the Philippine archipelago, support must account for displacement across separated terrain and islands while preserving continuity. The supported mission drives the positioning logic in the offense and defense examples.

  • Shifting fires and physically displacing the unit are different actions.
  • Check range, protection, signature, continuity, and sustainment when comparing alternatives.

Watch out: Assuming that the ability to support in varied terrain removes range or movement constraints.

Lesson source: page 18 · page 19 · page 29 · page 38

Weapon classification

The deck uses separate classification systems for caliber and transport. Light is 120 mm or less, medium is 121–160 mm, heavy is 161–210 mm, and very heavy starts at 211 mm. Transport categories are towed, self-propelled, and aerial. Identify the dimension the question asks about before applying the right classification.

  • A 155 mm weapon is medium by caliber under this lesson's bands.
  • Transport tells how the system moves, regardless of its caliber band.

Watch out: Selecting a caliber category based on whether the platform moves under its own power.

Lesson source: page 20 · page 21 · page 22 · page 23 · page 24

Four basic tasks

Field artillery supports forces in contact, supports the concept of operations, synchronizes fire support, and sustains field artillery operations. Contact support requires responsiveness when the supported force requests it. Support to the concept fulfills the maneuver commander's objective. Synchronization coordinates multiple available support assets to use resources economically and maximize effect. Sustainment keeps units able to continue operating.

  • Recall contact, concept, synchronization, and sustainment.
  • A single operation may need all four, with the question isolating a particular gap.

Watch out: Calling every coordinated fire mission support to forces in contact even when the case concerns synchronization across assets.

Lesson source: page 31

How the field artillery system fits together

Target acquisition detects, identifies, and locates targets. The fire direction center converts the observer's call for fire into fire commands, while coordination plans appropriate coverage with available weapons. Weapon and ammunition combinations deliver the intended effect. Survey provides a common grid and direction, meteorology supports accuracy, and communications connect the participants through voice and digital radio. Organization, logistics, mobility, and employment tactics support timely delivery over the operation.

  • Acquisition finds and locates; fire direction processes; the weapon and ammunition deliver.
  • Distinguish a common location reference from ballistic weather inputs and the communications link.

Watch out: Assuming that receiving a correct report proves it was translated into the correct command and delivered.

Lesson source: page 32 · page 33 · page 34 · page 35 · page 36 · page 37 · page 38

07Understand the reasoning

  • A simulated support request can be answered quickly and still miss the exercise objective if it is out of sequence with the supported unit's plan.
  • A delay in a single system element can reduce the usefulness of the whole response. Diagnose the information or coordination gap before assuming more equipment will solve it.
  • If a case says support was late, trace where the delay occurred before identifying the responsible system element.

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

How should the classroom staff compare moving immediately with waiting for a coordinated handover?

Recommend the sequence that meets the supported unit's highest priority and explain the effect of any remaining gap.

09Check yourself

Which basic task aligns artillery with other available fire-support assets?

Synchronize fire support.

How do target acquisition and fire direction differ?

Target acquisition supplies timely, accurate information. Fire direction processes it for coordinated delivery.
