01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Troop Leading Procedures turn the mission into a tested plan by protecting subordinate time, checking assumptions, and refining coordinated action.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Eight steps

Recall it, then reveal

Receive the mission; issue a warning order; make a tentative plan; initiate movement; conduct reconnaissance; complete the plan; issue the order; supervise and refine.

Why it matters Activities can overlap as information and preparation develop.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 5 · page 6 · page 7 · page 8 · page 9 · page 10 · page 11 · page 12 · page 13

02

Reverse planning and warning

Recall it, then reveal

Work backward from the required ready time; the sample uses about one-third for company planning and orders and two-thirds for subordinate preparation, supported by an early warning order.

Why it matters Waiting for every detail reduces the time subordinates have to prepare.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 5 · page 6 · page 16

03

Mission analysis

Recall it, then reveal

Separate specified tasks, implied tasks, the essential task, and constraints; restate the mission with unit, task, place, time, and purpose linked to higher intent.

Why it matters Facts about the mission and METT-TC must lead to deductions that change a planning decision.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 8

04

Courses of action

Recall it, then reveal

Develop task-and-purpose relationships around a decisive point, war-game critical events, and compare tradeoffs; the matrix informs the commander's judgment.

Why it matters A high score does not resolve a critical event with no workable response.

Lesson source: page 8 · page 9 · page 10

05

Reconnaissance and orders

Recall it, then reveal

Assign each consequential information gap a method, responsible leader, and time; integrate findings into Situation, Mission, Execution, Sustainment, and Command and Signal, including a company PACE sequence.

Why it matters Reconnaissance needs a decision to inform, and the order needs usable coordinating details.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 11 · page 12

06

Supervise and refine

Recall it, then reveal

Use backbriefs to check plans, rehearsals to test coordination, and inspections to check readiness; correct failed assumptions and verify the changes.

Why it matters A written plan is incomplete as a readiness claim until critical preparations and interactions are checked.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 5 · page 13

02Don’t confuse these

Specified task

/

Implied task

A specified task is explicitly assigned in the higher order; an implied task is inferred as necessary to accomplish the mission.

Lesson source: page 7

Warning order

/

Full order

The warning order passes available mission information and initial preparation tasks early; the full order presents the integrated plan and coordinating details.

Lesson source: page 5 · page 6 · page 12

Backbrief

/

Rehearsal

A backbrief reveals how subordinates understand and plan their responsibilities; a rehearsal tests how coordinated actions work together.

Reasoning Two-thirds of nine hours is six hours.

Analytical · 03Constraint-analysis style: A classroom planning option depends on closing a route before higher headquarters releases it from civilian use. What must change?Think first · reveal answer

Answer Treat the release requirement as a constraint and revise the action and timing to remain within it.

Reasoning A higher restriction affects the option's feasibility even if other preparation can continue.

05Know the facts

Eight steps
Receive the mission, issue a warning order, make a tentative plan, initiate movement, conduct reconnaissance, complete the plan, issue the order, and supervise and refine.
Reverse planning
Work backward from the ready time. The one-third/two-thirds guideline preserves roughly two-thirds of available time for subordinate planning and preparation.
Warning order
Gives subordinates enough known information to start preparing and identifies when and where to expect the full order, when known.
Mission analysis
Identify specified tasks, infer necessary implied tasks, and restate the mission with a purpose tied to the higher objective.
Reconnaissance and refinement
Check information gaps that could alter the tentative plan. Use backbriefs, rehearsals, and inspections to test the completed plan and correct problems.

06Build your understanding

Steps 1–2: Receive the mission, plan time, and issue a warning order

Anchor the timeline on the higher headquarters' required prepared time and work backward through orders, movement, preparation, rehearsals, and checks. The example uses one-third for the company planning and order window and leaves about two-thirds for subordinates. An early company warning order gives the tentative mission, order time and place, participating units, initial tasks outside SOP, and a timeline. Pass changes as confirmed details affect preparations.

  • Work backward from the required ready time and include higher headquarters' deadlines.
  • Give subordinates enough confirmed information to begin work promptly.

Watch out: Waiting for the complete order before issuing initial tasks or using the warning order only to repeat higher headquarters.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 5 · page 6 · page 16

Step 3: Analyze the mission and make deductions

Start with higher intent and separate specified tasks, implied tasks, the essential task, and constraints. The essential task and purpose inform the restated mission. The sample derives its purpose from the battalion intent and includes the unit, task, place, and time. For each METT-TC factor, draw a consequence that affects the plan. The sample links terrain and opposing approaches to decisions and treats civilian restrictions and support arrival times as real constraints.

  • Specified comes from the order. Implied enables the mission and must be inferred.
  • Explain how each fact changes a choice, requirement, or timing.

Watch out: Copying definitions and background while leaving the plan unchanged.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 8

Step 3: Develop, war-game, and compare courses of action

Build a course of action around a decisive point and explain why achieving the result there serves the purpose. Give each element a task and purpose and show how supporting efforts enable the main effort. War-game the critical events and check whether the plan has a response to each. Use relevant comparison factors to explain the tradeoffs. The decision matrix informs the commander, who still decides.

  • A decisive point is useful when the plan explains how it enables the mission.
  • Use war-game findings to refine the comparison and the selected course of action.

Watch out: Choosing a course of action solely because of its matrix total.

Lesson source: page 8 · page 9 · page 10

Steps 4–5: Initiate movement and conduct reconnaissance

The company starts authorized movement and preparation before the full order is issued to preserve time in the sector. Reconnaissance answers a specific gap in the tentative plan, such as whether terrain obstructs observation or whether a proposed approach is practical. The sample pairs each question with a method, responsible leader, and time. In the defensive example, viewing the area from the approaching force's side reveals issues a friendly map view may conceal.

  • Early movement and planning can overlap.
  • State the gap, verification method, leader, and time.

Watch out: Writing 'conduct reconnaissance' without defining the decision the report will inform.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 5 · page 11

Steps 6–7: Complete the plan and issue the order

Integrate reconnaissance findings before issuing the order. The sample uses Situation, Mission, Execution, Sustainment, and Command and Signal. It places the restated mission and purpose in Mission, coordinated actions in Execution, supplies and casualty collection in Sustainment, and a company-specific PACE plan in Command and Signal. The communications sequence reflects the assessed threat and available means.

  • Keep the mission purpose and higher constraints visible through the order.
  • Give subordinates a usable primary, alternate, contingency, and emergency sequence.

Watch out: Pasting the battalion communications reference into the order without defining the company plan.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 11 · page 12

Step 8: Supervise, rehearse, and refine

Arrange preparation priorities so teams know which work comes first. Use subordinate backbriefs to compare assigned responsibilities and find gaps. Rehearse the events where groups must coordinate and test the planned communications means with users. Inspections and personal checks reveal whether preparation matches the order. If a check reveals a changed condition or failed assumption, refine the affected work and confirm the result.

  • Backbriefs check plans; rehearsals check coordinated actions; inspections check readiness.
  • Use the remaining time for the critical checks and rehearse likely failure points.

Watch out: Listing a rehearsal time while leaving the method, events, or participating teams unspecified.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 5 · page 13

07Understand the reasoning

  • The steps overlap in the worked example. Early warning and authorized preparation protect subordinate time while the leader continues to analyze the mission.
  • An information gap is worth pursuing when the answer could change a decision. Prioritize that gap over details that would only make the briefing longer.
  • The sample is a worked defensive solution. Its fictional locations, times, and communications identifiers illustrate reasoning and provide context for the steps.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A Philippine Army classroom group has three hours before a demonstration must be ready. The lead student is waiting for confirmation of one venue detail, but the other sections can already gather materials and assign roles.

What should the lead student communicate now, and how should the group use the remaining time?

Show the decision and reasoning

Issue a warning order with the purpose, known tasks, ready time, and expected full briefing. Let sections begin authorized preparation while someone verifies the venue detail. Aim to give subordinates about two hours to prepare and use the final checks to catch any effect of the venue change.

09How to practice

  1. On receiving the mission, work backward from the required ready time and budget subordinate preparation time.
  2. Issue a warning order with known tasks, immediate preparation, and the next briefing time if available.
  3. Develop a tentative plan from the mission and compare feasible courses of action.
  4. Begin authorized movement or other early preparation as applicable and verify the information gaps that could change the plan.
  5. Complete and issue the order, including the coordinating information subordinates need.
  6. Use backbriefs, checks, and rehearsals to supervise preparation and refine the plan.

10Check yourself

What is the difference between a specified task and an implied task?

A specified task appears in the higher order. An implied task is inferred as necessary to carry it out.

What does the one-third/two-thirds guideline protect?

Time for subordinate leaders and their teams to plan and prepare.

11Objective & analytical practice

Answer first, then reveal the explanation. Situations are fictional classroom exercises applying the lesson.

objectiveA company has nine hours until the required prepared time. Under the sample guideline, how much time is planned for subordinates?

Answer: About six hours.

One-third gives the company about three hours for planning and issuing its order, leaving approximately two-thirds for subordinate preparation.

Lesson source: page 5 · page 16

objectiveWhat distinguishes a specified task from an implied task?

Answer: The higher order explicitly assigns a specified task. An implied task follows from what is needed to accomplish the mission.

The sample keeps both separate from the essential task and the constraints.

Lesson source: page 7

analyticalHigher headquarters requires a route to remain open for civilians until release. The company has a proposed action that depends on closing it early. How should the plan change?

Answer: Treat the release requirement as a constraint and revise the action so preparation can continue within it.

The civil situation and higher restriction affect timing and the feasibility of the course of action.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 8 · page 9

analyticalA comparison matrix favors a course of action, but the war-game finds a critical event with no response. What should the commander do before choosing it?

Answer: Refine the course of action against the critical event and revisit the comparison.

The matrix supports the commander's judgment, and the unanswered event may prevent the plan from achieving its purpose.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 10

analyticalThe company can start authorized work now, but the proposed position's visibility has only been estimated from the map. What pair of actions preserves preparation time and tests the assumption?

Answer: Start preparation that does not depend on the position and assign a leader to check and report actual visibility by a stated time.

The sample overlaps movement with planning and ties reconnaissance to an information gap.

Answer: Command and Signal.

The sequence belongs with the communications plan that supports execution.
