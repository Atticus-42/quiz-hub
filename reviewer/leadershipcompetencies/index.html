01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Leading influences people, developing builds future capability, and achieving turns priorities into monitored mission results.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Definition of leadership

Recall it, then reveal

Leadership influences people through purpose, direction, and motivation to accomplish the mission and improve the organization.

Why it matters The definition requires both present mission accomplishment and organizational improvement.

Lesson source: page 12 · page 13 · page 14 · page 16 · page 18

02

Four leading subsets

Recall it, then reveal

Leading comprises leads others, extends influence beyond the chain of command, leads by example, and communicates.

Why it matters Influence can depend on authority, cooperation, visible conduct, or shared understanding.

Lesson source: page 20 · page 21 · page 23

03

Influence beyond authority

Recall it, then reveal

Trust, negotiation, conflict resolution, and recognition of influence limits help gain cooperation outside the direct chain of command.

Why it matters A leader cannot assume another unit's personnel are direct subordinates.

Lesson source: page 20 · page 21 · page 23

04

Three developing subsets

Recall it, then reveal

Developing means creating a positive environment, preparing oneself, and developing others.

Why it matters Coaching and observed practice distribute capability beyond the most experienced soldier.

Lesson source: page 26 · page 29

05

Achieving results

Recall it, then reveal

Set direction and priorities, plan and execute, then monitor results and correct performance gaps while preserving longer-term capability.

Why it matters Activity counts alone cannot establish that the required result was achieved.

Lesson source: page 31 · page 32 · page 34

05Know the facts

Leadership
Influencing people through purpose, direction, and motivation to accomplish the mission and improve the organization.
Leading
Lead others and by example, communicate for shared understanding, and extend influence within its practical limits.
Developing
Create a positive environment, prepare yourself for future challenges, and build others' capabilities.
Achieving
Set direction and priorities, plan and execute, and monitor performance against mission and longer-term objectives.
Attributes and competencies
Attributes describe qualities a leader brings to the task. Competencies describe abilities applied through action.

06Build your understanding

Leadership as influence and action

Leadership uses purpose, direction, and motivation to accomplish the mission and improve the organization. Competency concerns the leader's ability to act effectively. Values, attributes, and professional knowledge provide a foundation, and the leader demonstrates competence by applying them in a situation. The lesson groups those actions into leading, developing, and achieving.

  • Purpose explains why; direction organizes action; motivation sustains effort.
  • Judge competencies by what the leader does and the result the action serves.

Watch out: Assuming professional knowledge alone demonstrates effective leadership.

Lesson source: page 12 · page 13 · page 14 · page 16 · page 18

Leading people within and beyond authority

Leads others includes providing purpose and motivation, enforcing standards, and balancing mission and soldier welfare. Extending influence beyond the chain of command requires trust, an understanding of the limits of one's influence, and skills such as negotiation and conflict resolution. The difference matters when the company needs another unit's cooperation and the leader has no direct command authority over it.

  • Use legitimate authority and standards within the unit.
  • Build agreement and trust beyond direct authority.

Watch out: Trying to direct another unit as if its personnel report to you.

Lesson source: page 20 · page 21 · page 23

Example and communication

The leader conveys influence through personal example and communication. Example makes character and competence observable. Communication involves listening actively, stating goals for action, and ensuring shared understanding. If soldiers can repeat the words yet disagree about what to do, shared understanding remains incomplete.

  • Demonstrate the standard through your own conduct.
  • Check that listeners interpret the goal consistently.

Watch out: Equating transmission of instructions with shared understanding.

Lesson source: page 20 · page 21 · page 23

Developing people and the organization

Development improves future capability while serving short- and long-term goals. A positive environment supports learning. Preparing oneself builds readiness for new responsibilities, while developing others builds capability across the company. The end state is an organization better prepared for the next challenge.

  • Recall create a positive environment, prepare self, and develop others.
  • Use coaching and practice to give subordinates a chance to perform.

Watch out: Doing every difficult task personally because it saves time today.

Lesson source: page 26 · page 29

Achieving results and sustaining capability

Achieving begins with objectives and priorities and continues through planning, preparing, executing, and monitoring. The leader reinforces strong performance and corrects gaps to improve how the organization works. The short-term result must coexist with a longer-term view of the company's ability to sustain performance.

  • Set direction and priorities, develop and execute a plan, and accomplish the mission.
  • Compare observed results with the objective and act on the gap.

Watch out: Counting activity without checking whether it produced the required result.

Lesson source: page 31 · page 32 · page 34

07Understand the reasoning

  • A leader can meet today's deadline and still leave the unit dependent on one experienced person. Developing others addresses that future gap.
  • The same activity can serve several competencies. Classify it by its main purpose, such as listening to align the team or coaching to build lasting capability.
  • A leader's visible example and a leader's explanation both influence others. Classify the action by how it achieves that influence.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A Philippine Army company is preparing a classroom demonstration. One experienced soldier can finish every equipment handover quickly, but the junior team leaders have yet to perform a full handover themselves.

How can the company complete this demonstration and build capacity for the next one?

Show the decision and reasoning

Set a completion standard and protect the demonstration deadline. Have the experienced soldier model one handover, then assign a junior leader to run the next one with observed feedback. Review errors before allowing an independent handover. This develops the team while keeping the current task on track.

09How to practice

  1. State the immediate result and the capability the team should retain afterward.
  2. Give each participant a role and confirm the standard through a brief backbrief.
  3. Demonstrate the task, then have a learner perform it while an experienced soldier observes.
  4. Review the result against the standard and assign a follow-up practice opportunity for any recurring gap.

10Check yourself

Which competency includes preparing yourself and developing others?

Developing.

Why is monitoring performance part of achieving?

It lets the leader compare progress with the desired result and adjust priorities or execution.
