01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

PAM 6-0102 signal planning develops requirements alongside operational planning, allocates real capabilities by mission priority and validates support before publishing plans and orders.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Concurrent requirements development

Recall it, then reveal

Signal planning runs with MDMP, or TLP at the appropriate small-unit level, using command priorities, staff needs, higher/adjacent/subordinate coordination, SOPs and applicable CEOI/CESI.

Why it matters Requirements and dependencies can change with the operational plan and must be coordinated rather than copied unchanged.

Lesson source: page 27 · page 28

02

Real capability

Recall it, then reveal

Compare requirements with serviceable equipment, actual personnel fill and training, funds, commitments and reserves; authorized holdings are only a baseline.

Why it matters A listed asset or position can be unavailable and therefore cannot automatically be allocated.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29

03

Priority under shortage

Recall it, then reveal

When resources cannot meet all requirements, prioritize by supportability and importance to mission accomplishment.

Why it matters An important requirement still needs an executable allocation, while a supportable low-priority task should not silently displace mission-critical support.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29

04

Disruption procedures

Recall it, then reveal

Plan redundant communications, repair or replacement and technical support for interrupted connectivity, with measures addressing EW, cyber incidents and compromised frequencies.

Why it matters Continuing priority traffic and restoring the original means are related tasks requiring workable resources.

Lesson source: page 29

05

Tentative plan

Recall it, then reveal

Allocate resources, outline primary, alternate, contingency and emergency support, and consider facility locations, security, interconnections and alternate routes.

Why it matters Demand, terrain, propagation, accessibility, staffing and support can make a map-based option impractical.

Lesson source: page 29

06

Final checks and publication

Recall it, then reveal

Validate site surveys, locations, coverage, resources, responsibilities, security and priorities; publish diagrams and instructions with plans and orders and coordinate the estimate and applicable annex material with operations staff.

Why it matters Publication communicates the validated arrangement but does not itself prove that coverage or support works.

Lesson source: page 30

02Don’t confuse these

Authorized resources

/

Usable capability

Authorization describes the baseline entitlement; usable capability depends on serviceability, actual personnel, training, commitments, reserves and support.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29

Fallback support

/

Restoration of original means

A workable alternative can continue priority traffic during disruption; repair, replacement and technical support restore the interrupted means.

Lesson source: page 29

Tentative allocation

/

Final validation and publication

The tentative plan proposes allocations, sites and fallback arrangements; final checks confirm practical coverage and responsibilities before the diagrams and instructions are published.

05Know the facts

Concurrent planning
Signal planning proceeds alongside the military decision-making process (MDMP) or troop leading procedures (TLP) at the appropriate level.
Requirements inputs
Use the commander’s priorities, staff and unit coordination, standard operating procedures, and applicable operating and standing instructions.
Resources and priorities
Check equipment readiness, actual personnel fill, training and funds. When resources are short, prioritize by supportability and importance to the mission.
Recovery procedures
Plan for interrupted connectivity and other disruptions, including redundant service, repair or replacement, and technical support.
Tentative plan
Allocate resources to requirements and outline primary, alternate, contingency and emergency support options.
Final checks and publication
Confirm coverage, responsibilities, resources, security and alignment with priorities. Publish the required diagrams and instructions with the plans and orders.

06Build your understanding

Develop requirements alongside operational planning

The signal planning process runs with MDMP, or with TLP at small-unit level. The commander gives priorities, staff describe user needs, and higher, adjacent and subordinate units provide guidance on dependencies and support. SOPs and applicable CEOI and CESI inform what the planner can employ.

  • Clarify each requirement and whose decision or task relies on it.

Watch out: Reusing the previous plan without coordinating can omit a new user or priority.

Lesson source: page 27 · page 28

Check real capability and set priorities

Maintain a current picture of equipment, personnel and funds. Authorized equipment and staffing establish baselines, while serviceability, actual fill, training, existing commitments and reserves determine usable support. Compare requirements with capability and prioritize by supportability and importance to the assigned mission.

  • Identify any support gap and explain the effect on the mission.

Watch out: An authorized position or listed asset can be unavailable for the exercise.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29

Prepare for disruptions

For interrupted connectivity, consider redundant communications, repair or replacement and technical support. The handout separately calls for measures addressing electronic warfare, cyber incidents and compromised frequencies. Choose recovery procedures that address the actual failure and identify the support needed to carry them out.

  • Distinguish continuing priority traffic from restoring the original means.

Watch out: A backup that uses the failed resource may be unavailable at the same time.

Lesson source: page 29

Prepare the tentative plan

The tentative plan allocates resources and develops primary, alternate, contingency and emergency support. It also considers locations for facilities, security, interconnections and alternate routes. Site choices must account for demand, terrain, propagation, accessibility and support.

  • Check that each allocation and fallback can be staffed and supported.

Watch out: A tentative site selected from a map may have a coverage or access gap.

Lesson source: page 29

Finalize, then publish

Check the entire plan for practical support, resource adequacy, priorities, responsibilities and security. Confirm the site survey, locations and coverage gaps. Prepare plans and orders with diagrams and instructions, and coordinate the signal estimate and applicable annex material with operations staff.

  • Resolve unresolved requirements and assign responsibilities before publication.

Watch out: Diagrams help communicate a plan, but they do not validate coverage.

Lesson source: page 30

07Understand the reasoning

  • A requirement and an allocation answer different questions. The requirement describes what the unit needs and the allocation shows what available resources can actually support.
  • A backup that depends on the same failed equipment or unavailable operator may offer little continuity. Test fallback assumptions against the disruption they are meant to cover.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A fictional Philippine Army exercise has one trained operator available for two reporting desks. The first desk receives time-sensitive safety reports, while the second compiles routine attendance totals.

How should the plan allocate support and account for a temporary outage?

Show the decision and reasoning

Assign the trained operator to the safety desk and agree on a lower-demand schedule for attendance reporting. Document an authorized alternative for urgent safety messages and who will activate it. Before publication, rehearse the handoff and check that the remaining staff can carry it out.

09How to practice

  1. Gather support requirements from the commander’s guidance, staff users, and participating units.
  2. Verify equipment, staffing, training and other resources, then rank requirements by mission importance and supportability.
  3. Draft an allocation with assigned responsibilities and workable alternatives for interruptions.
  4. Check the draft against priority needs, coverage, actual resources and security requirements.
  5. Resolve remaining decisions with the operations staff and publish the authorized plan with its supporting material.
