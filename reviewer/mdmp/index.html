01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

The MDMP turns a mission into an approved order through seven steps, each handing its outputs to the next, with the commander driving, the Ex-O managing, and the staff contributing through running estimates.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Seven steps and warning orders

Recall it, then reveal

Receipt of mission; mission analysis; COA development; COA analysis (war game); COA comparison; COA approval; orders production, dissemination and transition. WARNORDs follow Steps 1, 2 and 6.

Why it matters Each step's outputs become the next step's inputs, and warning orders let subordinates plan in parallel.

Lesson source: page 3 · page 4

02

Time and the full or modified MDMP

Recall it, then reveal

Commanders keep one-third of available time and give at least two-thirds to subordinates. The full MDMP examines two or more COAs; a time-constrained staff modifies it, and a unit must master the full MDMP first.

Why it matters Time determines how much detail the plan can have.

Lesson source: page 4 · page 7 · page 12

03

Mission analysis

Recall it, then reveal

The most critical step. Sort specified, implied and essential tasks, constraints, facts and assumptions; set CCIRs (PIR and FFIR) and EEFIs; write the mission statement and the COA evaluation criteria.

Why it matters If the problem is not understood well, no good plan can address it.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 8 · page 9

04

COA development and war game

Recall it, then reveal

Screen COAs as feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable and complete. War-game by belt, avenue-in-depth or box method, without comparing COAs.

Why it matters The war game tests and refines each COA before the comparison in Step 5.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 10 · page 11

05

Comparison, approval and orders

Recall it, then reveal

In the decision matrix the lowest total is most desirable. The commander approves, modifies or rejects; the order is reconciled with its annexes, crosswalked with higher and adjacent orders, and handed to current operations.

Why it matters The matrix informs the commander's judgment and does not replace it.

Lesson source: page 11 · page 12

06

Signal officer's contribution

Recall it, then reveal

Through the C4S running estimate: assess network operations, spectrum management, network defense and information protection for each COA, put command posts and critical communications nodes on sketches, plan PACE, recommend EEFIs and prepare Annex H.

Why it matters C4S requirements are best added while they can still shape the COA.

02Don’t confuse these

Specified task

/

Implied task

A specified task is assigned by higher headquarters; an implied task is not stated but must be done to accomplish a specified task. An essential task is either kind that must be executed to accomplish the mission.

Lesson source: page 8

PIR / FFIR

/

EEFI

PIRs (enemy and environment) and FFIRs (friendly forces) are the two kinds of CCIR, which are information to collect. An EEFI is information to protect and is not a CCIR.

Lesson source: page 8 · page 9

Plans and orders reconciliation

/

Plans and orders crosswalk

Reconciliation is internal: the base order and annexes agree. The crosswalk is external: the order is compared with higher and adjacent orders for unity of effort.

Lesson source: page 12

05Know the facts

Seven steps
Receipt of mission, mission analysis, COA development, COA analysis (war game), COA comparison, COA approval, and orders production, dissemination and transition. The outputs of one step become the inputs of the next.
Warning orders
WARNORDs go out after Steps 1, 2 and 6 so subordinates can plan in parallel. Commanders allocate at least two-thirds of available time to subordinates and keep one-third.
Mission analysis
The most critical step. It produces the problem statement, mission statement, initial intent and planning guidance, initial CCIRs and EEFIs, updated IPB and running estimates, assumptions and COA evaluation criteria.
COA screening
Test each COA as feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable and complete. War-game it without comparing COAs, then compare against criteria fixed beforehand.
Signal officer's role
Contribute through the C4S running estimate: assess supportability in the war game, put command posts and critical communications nodes on sketches, plan PACE means, recommend EEFIs and write Annex H (Signal).

Topic 1 of 6

Foundations: definition, characteristics, and time

The MDMP is an iterative planning methodology to understand the situation and mission, develop a course of action, and produce an operation plan or order. It is one of three Army planning methodologies with Army design methodology and troop leading procedures. Its characteristics are collaborative, assessment starting in planning, driving preparation, working with Army design methodology, and iterative. The full MDMP examines two or more COAs; under time pressure the commander decides how to shorten it, and a unit must master the full process first.

  • WARNORDs follow Steps 1, 2 and 6, and at least two-thirds of available time goes to subordinates.
  • Time-saving techniques include more commander involvement, fewer COAs, parallel planning, collaboration and trusted liaison officers.

Watch out: Treating the MDMP as a one-way checklist or assuming shortening it needs no practice with the full process.

Lesson source: page 2 · page 3 · page 4 · page 7 · page 12

Topic 2 of 6

Staff roles, relationships, and products

The staff supports the commander, who remains the decision-maker and is responsible for everything the staff does or fails to do. The Ex-O is the principal assistant and staff integrator at battalion and brigade, and directs the staff planning timeline and quality control. Each section keeps a running estimate. S3 prepares Annexes A, C and M, S2 Annex B, S4 Annex F, S6 Annex H, S7 Annex I, and the engineer or chief of protection Annexes E and G.

  • Once the commander decides, staff officers support the decision even when it differs from their recommendation.
  • The S6 is the integrator for the mission command cell.

Watch out: Matching an annex to the wrong staff officer, especially Annex B (S2), Annex F (S4) and Annex H (S6).

Lesson source: page 5 · page 6 · page 13

Topic 3 of 6

Steps 1 and 2: Receipt of mission and mission analysis

Receipt of a mission, or its anticipation, triggers the MDMP and ends with the commander's initial guidance and WARNORD 1. Mission analysis is the most critical step. It analyzes the higher order, performs IPB, and separates specified, implied and essential tasks, constraints, facts and assumptions. CCIRs are PIRs and FFIRs, while EEFIs are information to protect. The mission statement answers who, what, when, where and why, and criteria for comparing COAs are set before the comparison.

  • A fact is true at a stated time; an assumption cannot be validated but is necessary for planning.
  • Essential tasks always appear in the mission statement.

Watch out: Treating an EEFI as a CCIR or setting evaluation criteria after seeing the COAs.

Lesson source: page 7 · page 8 · page 9

Topic 4 of 6

Step 3: Develop courses of action

A COA is a broad potential solution to an identified problem. Each COA is screened as feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable and complete. The staff assesses relative combat power, generates options starting with the decisive operation, arrays forces, develops a broad concept, assigns headquarters, and prepares a statement and sketch. The sketch shows boundaries, control measures, the main effort and the locations of command posts and critical communications nodes.

  • Historical minimum planning ratios include 3:1 for attacking a prepared or fortified position and 1:3 for defending one.
  • The S3 prepares the COA statement and sketch for each COA.

Watch out: Offering COAs that differ only in name, which fail the distinguishable test.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 10

Topic 5 of 6

Steps 4 and 5: War game and compare

War-gaming visualizes the flow of the operation through the action, reaction and counteraction cycle using the belt, avenue-in-depth or box method. Its rules require objectivity and forbid defending a COA because you developed it or comparing COAs. The Ex-O controls, the S3 plays the friendly commander and the S2 plays the enemy. COA comparison weighs COAs against the agreed criteria, and in a decision matrix the lowest total is the most desirable COA. If the staff cannot agree, the Ex-O decides which COA to recommend.

  • The matrix is a tool, not the decision, and rests on subjective judgments.
  • The belt method is the commander's usual preference when terrain has clear cross-compartments.

Watch out: Comparing COAs during the war game or choosing the highest matrix total.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 11

Topic 6 of 6

Steps 6 and 7 and the Signal officer's contribution

At COA approval the commander approves, modifies or rejects the COAs and issues final planning guidance, and the staff sends WARNORD 3. A modified or new COA is war-gamed before it is recommended. At orders production the COA statement becomes the concept of operations, the order is reconciled internally, crosswalked externally, approved, and handed to current operations. The Signal officer contributes through the C4S running estimate, assesses network operations, spectrum management, network defense and information protection, plans PACE means, recommends EEFIs and prepares Annex H.

  • Annexes are reviewed before the base order is signed.
  • The S6 plans communications for reports from collection assets to command posts.

Watch out: Confusing reconciliation (internal agreement of the order and annexes) with the crosswalk (external check with higher and adjacent orders).

Lesson source: page 11 · page 12 · page 13 · page 14

07Understand the reasoning

  • Time drives detail. The one-third / two-thirds rule and early warning orders protect subordinate planning time while the headquarters is still analyzing.
  • Criteria are set before COAs are compared, and war-gaming never compares COAs. This keeps the choice from being biased by the COA a planner developed or by what the commander is thought to want.
  • A decision matrix informs the commander's judgment. The lowest total is the most desirable COA, but the matrix rests on subjective ranks and criterion-by-criterion comparison is often more useful.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A Philippine Army battalion staff in a classroom exercise has just received a brigade order with 48 hours before execution. The S3 has finished the first draft of the problem statement, and the S6 has found that the only route for a relay site is covered by a no-fire area set by the brigade.

How should the S6 and the staff handle the no-fire area, and how much of the 48 hours should subordinate units get?

Show the decision and reasoning

Record the no-fire area as a constraint, because it is a restriction placed by higher headquarters. The S6 should report the resulting C4S shortfall in the mission analysis briefing, add the relay site and critical communications nodes to each COA sketch, and recommend protecting relay locations as an EEFI. Under the one-third / two-thirds rule the battalion keeps about 16 hours and gives subordinates about 32 hours, supported by an early warning order.

09How to practice

  1. Begin at receipt of mission: gather the order and products, update running estimates, assess time and issue the initial guidance and WARNORD 1.
  2. In mission analysis, analyze the higher order, perform initial IPB, sort tasks, constraints, facts and assumptions, and produce the mission statement, intent, planning guidance and evaluation criteria.
  3. Develop several COAs that pass the screening criteria and give each a statement and sketch showing the main effort and critical communications nodes.
  4. War-game each COA, record results on the synchronization matrix, and let each staff officer test the COA from his or her function without comparing COAs.
  5. Compare the COAs against the agreed criteria with a decision matrix, then brief the commander so he or she can approve, modify or reject.
  6. Turn the approved COA into the order, reconcile it with its annexes, crosswalk it with higher and adjacent orders, and hand it over to current operations.

10Check yourself

Which steps are followed by a warning order?

Step 1 (receipt of mission), Step 2 (mission analysis) and Step 6 (COA approval).

What do the S3, S2 and Ex-O do in the war game?

The S3 plays the friendly maneuver commander, the S2 plays the enemy commander and other threat groups, and the Ex-O is the unbiased controller.

Which annex does the S6 prepare, and to what does the S6 give input?

Annex H (Signal); input goes to Annex C and Appendix 12 (Cyberspace Electromagnetic Activities).

11Objective & analytical practice

Answer first, then reveal the explanation. Situations are fictional classroom exercises applying the lesson.

objectiveAn order arrives 72 hours before execution. How is time divided under the one-third / two-thirds rule?

Answer: About 24 hours for the battalion and about 48 hours for subordinate companies.

Commanders keep one-third for their own headquarters and give at least two-thirds to subordinates for planning and preparation.

Lesson source: page 7

objectiveName the five COA screening criteria.

Answer: Feasible, acceptable, suitable, distinguishable and complete.

Each COA is tested against these criteria before it continues to analysis.

Lesson source: page 9

objectiveIn a decision matrix, which total indicates the most desirable COA?

Answer: The lowest total.

Each COA is ranked against each criterion with 1 as best, multiplied by the weight and added across.

Lesson source: page 11

objectiveWhat does PACE stand for in signal planning?

Answer: Primary, alternate, contingency and emergency means of communication.

The S6 plans these means for each requirement and shows how each COA changes them.

Lesson source: page 2 · page 14

analyticalDuring a war game, an officer wants to settle which COA is best right now. What should the controller do?

Answer: Hold the comparison for Step 5 and keep recording advantages and disadvantages.

War-gaming rules forbid comparing COAs during the war game, and the comparison uses criteria agreed in advance.

Lesson source: page 10 · page 11

analyticalA COA sketch shows boundaries and the main effort but no command post or communications node. What should the S6 do?

Answer: Make sure command posts and critical communications nodes are added and node movement times are set in the war game.

The sketch is where supportability of communications is first visible, and the S6 should raise gaps while they can still shape the COA.

Lesson source: page 9 · page 14

analyticalThe commander modifies a COA at approval. What must happen before the staff recommends it?

Answer: War-game the modified COA, then issue final planning guidance and WARNORD 3.

A modified or new COA is war-gamed before the staff recommends it.

Lesson source: page 11
