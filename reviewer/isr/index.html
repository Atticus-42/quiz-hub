01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

ISR turns collection into timely, credible assessments by matching platforms to information needs and managing quality, continuity, and authorized sharing.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Collection to assessment

Recall it, then reveal

Surveillance continuously observes a target; reconnaissance answers a specific military question; intelligence evaluates and combines information to produce an assessment.

Why it matters Collection becomes operationally useful through processing, exploitation, and dissemination tied to a decision.

02

Four platform families

Recall it, then reveal

ISR platforms are airborne, ground-based, maritime, and space-based; examples include aircraft, ground radars or sensor networks, ships or submarines, and space systems.

Why it matters Platform family describes where the platform operates rather than the intelligence discipline it supports.

Lesson source: page 31 · page 32 · page 35 · page 36 · page 37 · page 38 · page 39 · page 40 · page 41

03

Intelligence disciplines

Recall it, then reveal

IMINT concerns imagery, SIGINT concerns signals, and MASINT uses measured distinctive characteristics or signatures.

Why it matters Identify the information characteristic rather than guessing from the collection platform.

Lesson source: page 41 · page 42 · page 43

04

Timeliness, accuracy, and fusion

Recall it, then reveal

Timeliness delivers information before the decision; accuracy limits errors through reliable information; fusion combines sources into a fuller assessment.

Why it matters Times and source reliability can explain conflicting reports, while uncertainty still needs to be stated.

Lesson source: page 49 · page 50 · page 51

05

Coverage, persistence, flexibility

Recall it, then reveal

Coverage concerns the observed area or objective; persistence concerns observation over time; flexibility redirects assets or methods as requirements change.

Why it matters A wide-area pass can cover the whole route and still leave long observation gaps.

Lesson source: page 52 · page 53 · page 54

06

Security, interoperability, ethics

Recall it, then reveal

Security protects information and assets; interoperability lets authorized teams exchange and use products; ethics guides collection, analysis, and dissemination within applicable law and principles.

Why it matters Technical access does not establish permission to share restricted information.

Lesson source: page 55 · page 56 · page 57

05Know the facts

Intelligence
An assessment produced by combining and evaluating information from surveillance, reconnaissance, and other sources.
Surveillance
Sustained observation of an area, activity, or subject of interest.
Reconnaissance and assets
Reconnaissance collects data for a specific military question. The lesson groups ISR assets into airborne, ground-based, maritime, and space-based systems.
Coverage and persistence
Coverage describes what portion of the area or activity can be observed. Persistence describes how long observation can be maintained.
Fusion and interoperability
Fusion integrates observations from multiple sources. Interoperability lets different teams and systems exchange and use the resulting information.
Quality and safeguards
Timeliness, accuracy, and flexibility help keep information useful. Security and ethics guide collection, analysis, and sharing.

06Build your understanding

ISR connects collection to decisions

ISR integrates sensors, assets, and the processing, exploitation, and dissemination that make collection useful for operations. Surveillance is continuous observation of a target, reconnaissance collects information to answer a specific military question, and intelligence combines the results with other data to produce assessments. The purpose is to improve a commander's understanding of conditions, threats, and likely developments before a decision.

  • Identify the commander's question and decision time.
  • Distinguish observed data from a processed assessment.

Watch out: Using the number of reports or sensor feeds as proof that commanders received usable intelligence.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 22 · page 23 · page 24 · page 26 · page 27 · page 28 · page 29

Airborne and ground-based platforms

Airborne ISR includes unmanned aerial vehicles, manned reconnaissance aircraft, and high-altitude long-endurance platforms. The lecture uses HALE for high-altitude observation over many hours. Ground-based ISR includes fixed or vehicle-mounted surveillance radars and fixed or mobile sensor networks. Use the task to determine the need for sustained observation, local coverage, or an airborne vantage point.

  • Classify the platform separately from the sensor or product it carries.
  • Match duration and position to the collection requirement.

Watch out: Assuming that one airborne platform is best for every geographic or time requirement.

Lesson source: page 31 · page 32 · page 35 · page 36 · page 37 · page 38

Maritime and space-based collection

Surface ships and submarines are maritime platforms. Space-based systems can provide wide-area imagery and signals collection, including in denied areas, but an orbital revisit pattern can leave gaps. The deck identifies imagery intelligence, signals intelligence, and measurement and signature intelligence. Commercial imagery may provide shareable unclassified products for multinational partners when appropriate.

  • A large space-based footprint may still have gaps in time.
  • SIGINT concerns signals, IMINT concerns imagery, and MASINT identifies distinctive measured signatures.

Watch out: Inferring continuous observation from the ability to see a broad area.

Lesson source: page 39 · page 40 · page 41 · page 42 · page 43 · page 44 · page 45

Timeliness, accuracy, and fusion

Timeliness requires collection, processing, and dissemination in time to inform the decision. Accuracy requires reliable information and limits errors and misinterpretation. Fusion draws on multiple sources, such as SIGINT, HUMINT, and IMINT, to form a more complete assessment. A timestamp and confidence assessment help the receiver judge whether apparently conflicting reports describe different conditions or an actual discrepancy.

  • Ask when the information was observed and when it is needed.
  • Compare sources before treating an inference as confirmed.

Watch out: Presenting agreement between two copies of the same report as independent corroboration.

Lesson source: page 49 · page 50 · page 51

Coverage, persistence, and flexibility

Coverage asks which geographic area or operational objective is observed. Persistence asks how continuously it is observed over time. Flexibility lets the organization redirect assets, adjust priorities, or change collection methods when the question changes. A broad pass may satisfy a coverage goal while a gap between passes leaves persistence inadequate.

  • Coverage is about the area or objective; persistence is about time.
  • Retask when the commander's information need changes.

Watch out: Adding geographic breadth when the actual gap is a recurring break in observation.

Lesson source: page 52 · page 53 · page 54

Security, interoperability, and ethics

Security protects sensitive ISR information and assets from compromise or exploitation. Interoperability enables coordination across services and partners so authorized participants can make use of the products. Ethics guides collection, analysis, and dissemination within applicable law, rules of engagement, and moral principles. In a sharing case, identify which product is useful, who can receive it, and how those users can interpret it.

  • Separate permission to share from the technical ability to exchange information.
  • Use relevant authorized products in formats partners can use.

Watch out: Assuming that technical access gives all participants permission to receive every source.

Lesson source: page 55 · page 56 · page 57

07Understand the reasoning

  • A report can be accurate when collected and too old to support the current decision. Check what could have changed since observation.
  • Two summaries based on one observation share the same underlying evidence. Independent confirmation can add confidence when the decision warrants it.
  • If a case has more data but no better decision, look for a gap in timing, accuracy, fusion, or dissemination.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

During a Philippine Army humanitarian assistance tabletop exercise, an older aerial image shows a road open, while a recent ground report says floodwater has reached it. The classroom staff must choose which route to include in a relief convoy briefing.

How should the staff reconcile the reports before recommending a route?

Show the decision and reasoning

Compare the observation times, confirm they refer to the same road segment, and seek a current authorized status update if time allows. The aerial image gives useful context, while the newer local report may better describe changing conditions. State the remaining uncertainty and give the staff a usable alternative if the road cannot be confirmed.

09How to practice

  1. Write the decision question and the deadline for answering it.
  2. Compare source coverage, freshness, and ability to confirm the detail that matters.
  3. Cross-check conflicting reports against an independent source when available.
  4. State the assessed answer, its confidence limits, and who is authorized to receive it.

10Check yourself

Which ISR component seeks an answer to a specific question?

Reconnaissance.

Why do timeliness and accuracy need to be assessed separately?

An accurate observation can arrive too late or become outdated, and a fast report can still be wrong.

objectiveHow do surveillance and reconnaissance differ?

Answer: Surveillance is continuous observation of a target. Reconnaissance gathers information to answer a specific military question.

The difference is the purpose of collection, although both can contribute to an intelligence assessment.

analyticalWide-area imagery shows every part of a route in one pass, but activity during long revisit gaps is missed. What gap remains?

Answer: Persistence.

Geographic coverage was achieved on the pass. Sustained monitoring over time is the missing capability.

Answer: Compare times, locations, and source reliability, combine the findings, and state what remains uncertain.

The process balances timeliness, accuracy, and fusion. Different observation times may explain the discrepancy.
