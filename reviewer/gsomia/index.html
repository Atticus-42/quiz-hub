01The essentials

THE LESSON IN ONE IDEA

Protected partner information requires authorized access and release, secure handling throughout its lifecycle and prompt reporting of suspected compromise.

Explain each point in your own words. Then use the lesson page to check the details.

01

Agreement purpose

Recall it, then reveal

A General Security of Military Information Agreement protects military information exchanged between partners; exact exchange obligations depend on the applicable agreement.

Why it matters Partner cooperation creates protection obligations without automatically authorizing every release.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 20 · page 23 · page 24 · page 25

02

Classification and marking

Recall it, then reveal

Sensitivity and potential consequences of unauthorized disclosure inform classification; markings communicate the resulting handling requirements.

Why it matters The classification decision and the visible marking have related but different functions.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 30

03

Individual access and partner release

Recall it, then reveal

Individual access requires appropriate clearance and demonstrated need to know; partner release also requires authorization, an approved recipient and channel, and documentation.

Why it matters Eligibility to see information and authority to transfer it are separate checks.

Lesson source: page 22 · page 26 · page 33 · page 34 · page 35 · page 36

04

Lifecycle protection

Recall it, then reveal

Use suitable secure storage, approved protected transmission and required secure disposal, with physical controls as well as technical protection.

Why it matters Encryption in transit does not fix exposure before transmission or after use.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 30 · page 31 · page 32 · page 37 · page 45

05

Suspected compromise

Recall it, then reveal

Promptly notify the responsible authority, record the affected material and facts, and follow authorized containment and recovery; later analysis supports prevention.

Why it matters Credible suspicion warrants initial reporting before the full investigation is complete.

Lesson source: page 27 · page 38 · page 39

06

Accountability and assurance

Recall it, then reveal

Access records, exchange records, audits, inspections and role-specific training help verify that handling controls work and identify improvements.

Why it matters A signed policy establishes direction, while records and observed practice show whether it is followed.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29 · page 40 · page 41 · page 42 · page 44 · page 45 · page 46

02Don’t confuse these

Clearance

/

Need to know

Clearance establishes appropriate eligibility; need to know establishes a legitimate requirement for the particular information. Both are required for individual access.

Lesson source: page 22 · page 33 · page 36

Secure channel

/

Authorized release

A protected channel safeguards transmission; release authorization establishes whether the information may be sent to that recipient at all.

Lesson source: page 35 · page 36 · page 37

Confidentiality

/

Integrity

Confidentiality concerns who may see information; integrity concerns its accuracy and completeness.

05Know the facts

GSOMIA
A General Security of Military Information Agreement sets protections for military information exchanged between partners. Specific terms depend on the applicable agreement.
Classification and handling
Sensitivity and the effect of unauthorized disclosure inform classification. Markings and applicable rules govern storage, transmission and disposal.
Clearance and need to know
Access to classified information requires appropriate clearance and a legitimate need for the particular information.
Controlled sharing
Verify authorization for the specific release and use an approved recipient, channel and documented process.
Accountability and review
Records of access and exchange support review. Role-based permissions, training and audits help sustain appropriate handling.
Suspected compromise
Report credible suspicion promptly through required channels and document the material affected and actions taken.

06Build your understanding

Shared obligations and trust

The lesson describes GSOMIA as protection for sensitive military information exchanged by partners. Both sides help protect it, with controls that preserve confidentiality and integrity and records supporting accountability. The exact requirements for an actual exchange depend on the applicable agreement.

  • Confidentiality concerns who can see information. Integrity concerns its accuracy and completeness.

Watch out: Cooperation with a partner does not by itself authorize a particular release.

Lesson source: page 19 · page 20 · page 23 · page 24 · page 25

Protection across the information lifecycle

Marking helps users recognize sensitivity and handling requirements. Use secure facilities or systems for storage, approved protected channels for transmission and the required secure disposal method when material is no longer retained. Physical controls matter even where a system uses technical protection.

  • Identify the stage of handling before selecting a protection.

Watch out: Encryption during transmission cannot correct insecure storage or disposal.

Lesson source: page 21 · page 30 · page 31 · page 32 · page 37 · page 45

Access and release are separate decisions

Individual access depends on appropriate clearance and a demonstrated need for the information. Role-based permissions help enforce duty-based access. A partner release also requires authorization under the applicable process, an approved recipient and channel, and documentation of the exchange.

  • Use an audit trail to determine who accessed an item, when and for what purpose.

Watch out: A secure transmission can still be an unauthorized disclosure.

Lesson source: page 22 · page 26 · page 33 · page 34 · page 35 · page 36

Suspected compromise and response

Notify the responsible authorities immediately when credible facts indicate a possible breach. Record the incident, the material involved and steps taken. Containment limits the effect, recovery helps restore secure handling, and forensic analysis helps establish the cause and guide prevention.

  • Prompt notice and follow-up documentation both support response.

Watch out: Waiting for a complete investigation can delay the required report of a suspected breach.

Lesson source: page 27 · page 38 · page 39

Assurance and improvement

Audits compare practice with procedures. Logs, current records, inspections and role-specific training help show whether controls operate as intended. When reviews reveal recurring weaknesses, use feedback to update the procedure, control or training and check the changed practice.

  • Technical protections, physical controls and trained users cover different weaknesses.

Watch out: A signed policy alone does not show that personnel follow it.

Lesson source: page 28 · page 29 · page 40 · page 41 · page 42 · page 44 · page 45 · page 46

07Understand the reasoning

  • Partner status and an individual’s clearance answer different questions from whether that person needs a particular file or whether release has been authorized.
  • An approved channel protects a transmission. Recipient and release checks address whether the transfer should occur in the first place.

08Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A partner liaison in a fictional Philippine Army course requests the complete instructor packet shortly before a rehearsal. A separately approved handout covers the rehearsal, but the packet’s release status is unclear.

What can the coordinator do now while resolving the packet request?

Show the decision and reasoning

Confirm that the approved handout covers this audience and use the authorized channel to share it. Refer the packet request to the designated authority with the liaison’s role and stated need. This supports the rehearsal while the coordinator verifies whether the additional material may be released.

09How to practice

  1. Identify the exact item requested and confirm its markings and handling instructions.
  2. Verify the recipient’s role, need to know and authorization for the requested release under the applicable guidance.
  3. Confirm the approved channel and exact recipient before transmitting the authorized material.
  4. Record the exchange using the required process and route any suspected compromise promptly through designated channels.

Answer: Sensitivity and the potential effect of unauthorized disclosure.

Answer: Appropriate clearance and demonstrated need to know. The exchange must also be authorized.

Answer: Hold the transfer and obtain verification through the designated process.

Encryption protects the data in transit. It does not establish who may receive the information.
