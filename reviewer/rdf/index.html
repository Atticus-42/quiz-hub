01Quick briefing

Radio direction finding estimates the direction from which an RF signal arrives. Use that bearing to support a decision while preserving the difference between a direction and a position.

Read with Radio Direction Finder (Revised).

02Know the facts

Direction of arrival
DOA is the azimuthal arrival angle relative to the RDF system.
Phase comparison
Phase comparison, including the interferometric example, compares phase differences across antennas or antenna elements.
Time difference of arrival
TDOA compares when a signal arrives at spatially separated antennas.
Sources and applications
Possible sources include communications, radar and distress beacons. The lesson discusses navigation assistance, search and rescue, and spectrum monitoring.
Limits of a bearing
A single direction can inform coordination while distance, precise location and transmitter identity still require other evidence.

03Understand the reasoning

  • State the reference for a direction before a recipient uses it. A bearing given relative to a system needs context to support a shared understanding.
  • A bearing can guide what a rescue team verifies next. Keep a possible source association tentative until independent exercise information supports it.

04Apply it

Fictional Philippine Army classroom exercise

A Philippine Army rescue tabletop presents a single synthetic bearing associated with a possible distress beacon. The exercise map shows two landing points in that direction, and the team can verify only one immediately.

What should the team relay, and how should the coordinator use it?

Show the decision and reasoning

Relay the measured direction with its reference and keep both landing points open as possibilities. Use independent exercise reports and current access information to prioritize a check. The bearing narrows the discussion, but it cannot select one location or identify the beacon by itself.

05Check yourself

What does TDOA compare?

Differences in the time at which the same signal arrives at separated antennas.

Does one bearing establish an exact location?

No. It describes a direction, while a precise location requires additional evidence.
